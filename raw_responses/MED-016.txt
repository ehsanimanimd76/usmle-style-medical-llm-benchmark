Answer: B. Central retinal artery occlusion

Reasoning: The patient's presentation of sudden, painless vision loss with a pale retina and cherry-red spot at the macula is classic for central retinal artery occlusion (CRAO). The cherry-red spot results from absent retinal blood flow with preserved choroidal circulation. Atrial fibrillation is a risk factor for embolic CRAO. Retinal detachment does not produce a cherry-red spot, central retinal vein occlusion causes a blood-and-thunder appearance, optic neuritis is usually painful, and vitreous hemorrhage obscures the fundus. Thus, CRAO is the most likely diagnosis.
