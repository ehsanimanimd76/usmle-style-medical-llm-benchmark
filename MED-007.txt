Answer: A. Intravenous calcium gluconate

Reasoning: The patient presents with severe hyperkalemia (serum potassium 7.2 mEq/L), ECG changes (tall, peaked T waves and widened QRS), and profound muscle weakness. These findings indicate a life-threatening condition requiring immediate intervention to prevent cardiac arrest. The immediate priority is to stabilize the cardiac membrane. Intravenous calcium gluconate is the first-line treatment for acute hyperkalemia with ECG changes. It stabilizes the cardiac membrane by antagonizing the effects of potassium on cardiac cells. Insulin plus glucose drives potassium into cells but is not used alone due to the risk of hypoglycemia. Furosemide has limited efficacy in CKD. Sodium polystyrene sulfonate is not suitable for acute, severe hyperkalemia. Hemodialysis is definitive but is typically initiated after stabilizing the patient.
