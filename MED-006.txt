Answer: C. Start empiric intravenous antibiotics immediately

Reasoning: The patient presents with classic signs of bacterial meningitis (fever, headache, photophobia, neck stiffness, confusion) and a petechial rash, which strongly suggests meningococcal disease. Meningococcemia is a life-threatening condition requiring immediate antibiotic therapy to prevent progression to septic shock and multiorgan failure. Hemodynamic instability does not preclude starting antibiotics, as delays in treatment significantly increase mortality. Lumbar puncture is not performed immediately in unstable patients due to risks of increased intracranial pressure or bleeding. MRI is not urgent for this presentation. Acyclovir is for viral encephalitis, which is less likely here. Discharging her is inappropriate. Thus, empiric IV antibiotics is the most critical next step.
