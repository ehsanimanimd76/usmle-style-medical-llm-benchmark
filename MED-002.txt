Answer: A. Begin intravenous regular insulin immediately

Reasoning: The patient presents with classic signs of diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA): hyperglycemia (480 mg/dL), metabolic acidosis (pH 7.12, bicarbonate 10 mEq/L), and symptoms of nausea/vomiting. Insulin is the cornerstone of DKA management to reverse hyperglycemia, inhibit ketogenesis, and correct acidosis. While hypokalemia (3.0 mEq/L) is present, potassium replacement is typically initiated after insulin therapy to avoid paradoxical hypokalemia from insulin-induced intracellular shift. However, in the acute phase, insulin initiation is the immediate priority to stabilize the patient. Potassium replacement is also critical but is managed concurrently with insulin, not as a prerequisite. Thus, A is the most appropriate next step.
